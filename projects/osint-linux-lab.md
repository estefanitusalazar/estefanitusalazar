# OSINT Linux Lab

## Overview

Practical OSINT study/lab environment on Linux focused on collecting and validating publicly available information in an ethical, authorized context.

## Tools and purposes

- **Holehe** — check whether an email may be associated with online services.
- **Sherlock** — search for a username across many sites.
- **Maigret** — broader username enumeration and reporting.
- **theHarvester** — collect public domain-related data such as emails and subdomains.
- **GHunt** — inspect publicly exposed information related to Google ecosystem accounts when available.
- **SpiderFoot** — automate OSINT collection and relationship discovery.
- **Maltego** — visualize relationships between identities, domains, infrastructure and other entities.
- **ExifTool** — inspect metadata from images and documents.
- **WhatWeb** — identify web technologies.
- **Nmap** — authorized network/service reconnaissance.
- **Amass / Subfinder** — subdomain discovery for authorized domains.

## Suggested workflow

1. Start with a clearly authorized target or lab asset.
2. Use email/username tools to identify possible public profiles.
3. For domains, combine passive collection with technology/subdomain discovery.
4. Inspect metadata only on files you are allowed to analyze.
5. Organize findings and relationships.
6. Verify every result before drawing conclusions.

## Ethics and safety

The lab is explicitly intended for:

- your own accounts and infrastructure;
- CTFs and training environments;
- assets for which you have explicit authorization.

OSINT results can produce false positives or outdated information, so corroboration is essential.
